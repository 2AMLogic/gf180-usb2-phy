- **Result**:

  **1. The fault, and where it was.** `klt`'s layout-plan executor derives
  `connectivity[]` from the netlist digest's source/drain/gate terminals
  only: its `_TERMINAL_SUFFIXES` table has no bulk entry, and the module
  docstring calls the omission deliberate. A MOS body is therefore never in
  a plan net, so the contacted tap pads `mos_array` draws for gf180mcu's
  tap-distance rule (`TAP_PAD<k>` in its generator report, klayout-tools
  #4143) were never wired. Separately, the only text labels in the committed
  streams were the ones klt's own router writes -- one per net it drew a leg
  for, partially-routed nets included -- which is neither the schematic's
  port set nor a subset of it. Both were measured directly, not inferred:
  the label sets before and after are listed per block in the frozen
  `stream-comparison.txt`. Filed as a tool gap under the friction protocol:
  `2AMLogic/klayout-tools#5546`.

  **2. The repair, in the generator's existing post-plan pass.** Three
  additions, all after `execute_layout_plan_document()` returns, which is
  where the scripted routes already go -- no plan, and no GDS, is hand-edited:

  * `_netlist_pin_map()` re-reads the plan's own netlist digest (the object
    klt validated the plan against) and resolves **every** terminal of every
    placed device onto a generated port, including the one-pin nets the
    executor's `nets[]` omits because there is nothing to route. A terminal
    the generator's own suffix table does not map is a body; a group's body
    is tied through one of its `TAP_PAD*` ports. One pad per group is enough
    and is all that is drawn: a group's pads contact the one well (pfet) or
    the one substrate (nfet) its devices sit in, so they are one conductor
    before any metal. A group whose devices disagree on their body net is a
    reported problem, never a silent tie.
  * the scripted router now also re-draws any net that gained a tap pad,
    even one klt reported routed, because klt's own route reaches the device
    terminals only. A tap pad takes its via on the port point rather than
    inset: the pad is 0.42 um square and the via 0.26 um.
  * `_write_pin_labels()` drops every existing label on the three metal
    label purposes and writes one per `is_pin` net of the digest, on the
    metal-1 label purpose at a device terminal of that net.
