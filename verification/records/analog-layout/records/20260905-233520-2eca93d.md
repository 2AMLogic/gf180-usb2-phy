  1. **The committed plan and what it decides.** 78 devices in 24 groups,
     201.939 × 101.120 µm overall, one `rows[]` row at `spacing_um: 3.0`,
     `routing: {"layer_role": "metal", "width_um": 0.23}`.
     - The **six `ppolyf_u` ladder segments are six separate `res_array`
       groups**, because their `r_length` values are all different
       (11.43 / 0.343 / 0.686 / 1.371 / 2.743 / 5.486 µm). Each resolved
       its own `length_um` from the netlist, and the run emits **zero
       warnings** — where the superseded record's throwaway two-group probe,
       which lumped all six into one `res_array`, emitted five `length_um`
       disagreement warnings and drew all six at 11.43 µm. That is the
       single most load-bearing grouping decision in the plan.
     - Each of the six pull-up/trim switch banks is **one 10-wide
       `mos_array`** of the flattened `W=100 µm` fingers issue #56 created
       (`15.100 × 101.120 µm` per bank), which is the grouping those fingers
       exist to express.
     - The six control inverters are **per-device groups clustered in n/p
       pairs** at one end of the row. This is measured, not stylistic: with
       the twelve inverter devices instead lumped into one 6-wide nfet array
       plus one 6-wide pfet array, all six gate-input nets collapse onto the
       same adjacent-block pair and five of them lose to `crosses
       already-routed net 'PU_EN'` — 1/21 routed instead of 6/21.
  2. **6 of 21 nets route: `PU_EN`, `TRIM0`…`TRIM4`.** Every routed net is a
     two-block gate-input net between an inverter's own n/p pair. Every
     unrouted net spans three or more blocks, or has to bus along a switch
     bank's own port column, and fails for one of the three limitation
     classes `layout/README.md` already characterises: a same-drawing-layer
     self-net bus across a block's own ports, a backbone crossing an
     unrelated block's bbox (no obstacle avoidance), or a collision with an
     already-drawn net. `unmapped_netlist_nets` is empty — every netlist net
     was mapped to real ports; the failures are routing, not derivation.
  3. **DRC is clean — and the older blocks' violations are root-caused.**
     The committed plan's GDS reports `status: "clean"`,
     `violation_count: 0`. Two probes isolate why, against the *same* plan
     document with only `routing.width_um` varied:

     | Probe | `klt drc --deck gf180mcu` |
     |---|---|
     | `dplus_pullup` as committed (`width_um: 0.23`) | **0 violations** |
     | `dplus_pullup` forced to klt's default `width_um: 0.17` | **172 × `metal1.width.1`** |

     Placement, group count and routed-net count are identical in both (6/21)
     — the only difference is the drawn width of the routes. The `gf180mcu`
     deck declares `metal1.width.1` at 0.23 µm (DRM 7.13 "Mn.1"); klt's
     documented default routing width is 0.17 µm, so **every** segment a
     default-width route draws is below minimum. Three further probes
     confirm the same mechanism explains the older blocks, whose plans carry
     no `routing` block at all and therefore take that default:

     | Plan | as committed (default 0.17 µm) | forced to `width_um: 0.23` |
     |---|---|---|
     | `differential_receiver` | 19 × `metal1.width.1` | **0 violations** |
     | `se_receiver_dm` | 22 × `metal1.width.1` | **0 violations** |
     | `se_receiver_dp` | 22 × `metal1.width.1` | **0 violations** |

     Routed-net counts are unchanged (0/8, 0/9, 0/9) in all three, so this is
     purely a DRC-attribution correction. **This supersedes this repo's own
     prior diagnosis** — recorded in `layout/README.md` and in the two
     records before this one — that those violations were rejected-leg
     "mitered dead end" geometry. They are not: they are ordinary route
     segments drawn narrower than the deck's own minimum. That also explains
     why the upstream maintainer's source inspection found no miter
     construction while the symptom kept reproducing: the refutation was
     correct and the symptom was real, but the two were about different
     things. Correcting the three older plans is deliberately **out of this
     issue's scope** and is tracked as gf180-usb2-phy#65.
  4. **`routing.layer_role: "metal2"` was measured and rejected for the
     committed plan.** Routing the same plan on metal2 at 0.28 µm (the
     deck's metal2 minimum) removes the whole same-drawing-layer rejection
     class — the remaining failures become "the backbone plows through its
     own pin's block", i.e. klt still forbids running over a block even on a
     layer that cannot short to it — but does **not** raise the routed-net
     count above 6/21, and it introduces **284 × `via1.width.1`** violations,
     because `gen_compose`'s via-drop square is a hard-coded 0.22 µm while
     the deck's `via1.width.1` minimum is 0.26 µm. A DRC-dirty result with no
     routing gain is not a better plan, so the committed plan stays on
     metal1.
  5. **`orientation` was measured and left at its default.** Applying
     `mirror_x` to the pfet driver groups changes nothing (6/21, DRC clean);
     `mirror_y` and `rotate_180` move the gate ports to the far edge and drop
     the count to **0/21**. `"none"` is therefore the measured-best value
     here, not an unexamined default.
  6. **`spacing_um` was swept.** At `2.0` µm the result carries one
     `metal1.space.1` violation (a 0.20 µm gap between a route and a block's
     own metal); `2.5`, `3.0` and `4.0` µm are all clean at 6/21. The plan
     commits `3.0` — one step above the smallest value that clears, so the
     result is not sitting exactly on a cliff edge.
