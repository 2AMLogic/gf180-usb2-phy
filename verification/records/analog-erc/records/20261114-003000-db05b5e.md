### The two ties, and why each verdict is falsifiable

**`nwell_pos_tap` — drawn geometry, no assertion.** Every drawn `Nwell`
(`21/0`) shape must hold a tap that reaches the positive rail. The tap is
`Comp ∩ Nplus` (`tap_requires: ["32/0"]`) — the `Comp && Nplus` n-tap the klt
erc docs name for gf180mcu, and the same boolean the curated deck's own
`comp.tap_distance.nwell.*` rules use. The narrowing is load-bearing: a bare
`22/0` tap layer would match every PMOS source/drain contact in the same well
and klt would grade the declaration `skipped` /
`degenerate_tap_declaration` (klayout-tools#2199). It is in `checked` on all
five streams, in **no** assertion list — this half is an ordinary
geometrically-derived pass over 17 / 4 / 12 / 4 / 4 wells.

**`substrate_neg_tap` — an asserted region, and what makes it evidence.**
These are native-substrate blocks: `klt layers` finds no `LVPWELL` (`204/0`)
and no other tub layer anywhere in the five streams, unlike the digital
partition's standard-cell rows which do draw `204/0`. There is nothing drawn
to name on the well side, so the tie is declared `well_layer: null` +
`well_boxes` (klayout-tools#2255) and klt grades it under
`erc_coverage.checked_by_well_assertion`. The boxes are **derived from each
stream, not hand-typed** — `Comp − Nwell`, grouped by a 1.1 µm proximity
closure, one bounding box per group of diffusion — and
`scripts/analog_erc.py` re-derives them on every run and **refuses to run if
a committed spec has drifted**. Five measurements, all in the frozen summary:

## What is NOT established

The clean verdict is narrower than it looks. Each of these is measured, not
estimated.

- **Not every drawn body-tie pad is wired to the rail.** `klt erc` joins
  conductors only through the declared `stackup`/`vias`; it has no device
  recognition and does not model substrate conduction. The harness therefore
  counts what the model cannot see: of the drawn substrate tap pads — 84 / 28
  / 24 / 28 / 28 — **all** are contacted, but only **17 / 6 / 6 / 6 / 6**
  reach the ground rail through `Contact`/`Metal1`/`Via*`/`Metal2-3`. The
  rest sit on isolated `Metal1` pads and are tied only through the substrate
  itself, which is what `klt lvs`'s deck-based extraction sees (its
  `body_verification.status` reads `verified` on all five streams, where this
  ERC model cannot corroborate it). That is a layout observation about the
  generator, not a defect this item grades, and it is filed as its own
  follow-up issue. It is also why the asserted partition stops at device-group
  granularity: the 0.6 – 1.03 µm row of the gap table is the per-pad
  partition, and it reports 34 / 12 / 12 / 12 / 12 `erc.missing_tie` findings.
- **`erc.supply_short` is not computed.** Every run records it in
  `erc_coverage.inapplicable` with reason `substrate_continuity_not_modelled`
  (klayout-tools#4384): with two or more `kind: "supply"` nets declared, klt
  discloses that rails which are one conductor through a shared substrate
  read as separate clean islands, and asks for an LVS cross-check. Zero
  `erc.supply_short` findings here therefore means "the declared stack shows
  no short", not "no short exists". The cross-check is the LVS table above —
  each block's compare pairs the two supplies as **distinct** nets at
  `status: "match"`, which a short between them could not produce.
- **The severed-rail half is not measured.** `nets[].roles` is deliberately
  not declared: in all five streams the supplies share `Metal1`/`Metal2`/
  `Metal3` with every signal net, so no role is *owned* by a supply and
  claiming ownership would be false about the layout. The consequence is
  recorded rather than hidden — the met citation's
  `power_delivery.supply_unlabelled_islands` is `{}`, klt's honest "not
  measured", never a fabricated zero (klayout-tools#2510/#2524).
- **No antenna verdict.** `--pdk` is deliberately not passed, so the antenna
  half rolls `not_checked` on every stream. Item 11 grades `erc_status` and
  `erc_coverage`, and an antenna verdict is explicitly not its subject
  (klayout-tools#1994); adding `--pdk` would put an unrelated pass/fail into
  this item's citation for no gain to it. The analog partition has no antenna
  evidence either way, and this record makes no antenna claim.
- **Nothing about IR drop, EM, or latch-up margin.** Item 11 is the
  *structural* question ("is the supply connected to what it powers"), not
  the analysis question. `klt power` was not run.
- **Nothing about the n-well tie's electrical quality.** Every drawn n-well
  holds a tap reaching the positive rail; how many taps, how far apart, and
  what series resistance they present are not this check's subject. The PDK's
  own 20 µm tap-reach rule (DRM 7.5 Comp DF.13/DF.14) is a DRC matter.
- **The signoff manifest grades one of the five streams, not all five.** `klt
  signoff` resolves item 11's compound citation into exactly one `(erc, lvs)`
  pair — "a later part of the same kind replaces an earlier one", in its own
  words — so unlike item 4, which takes a per-block citation *list*, item 11
  has no multi-member form for a partition built from five separately
  signed-off streams. `manifests/gf180-usb2-phy.json` therefore cites a
  single visible pair (`differential_driver`, the largest stream: 17 wells,
  84 tap pads, 17 asserted polygons) rather than a ten-entry list the grader
  would silently reduce to one pair while rendering only two `parts`. The
  other four streams' evidence is this record and these artifacts, is pinned
  by hash in `provenance.inputs[]` above (so `verification/check_records.py`
  fails if any stream or spec moves without a fresh record), and is re-run by
  `scripts/analog_erc.py` whose shape-and-gating self-test runs in CI. The
  tool gap is filed upstream at `2AMLogic/klayout-tools`; see "Friction" in
  **Links**.
