# Record 20261009-123738-05087d5

- **Record ID**: 20261009-123738-05087d5
- **Claim**: a verification-only structural experiment for issue #123.
  The question is what an RX input synchronizer of the simplest form
  (two independent flops on `rxdp` and two on `rxdm`) does to
  `usb_utmi_phy`'s receive behaviour. The candidate sits in a test-only
  wrapper in front of the **unchanged** production RTL and is compared
  with the baseline (no added flops) on the existing independent-host
  grid, under paired-input skew, under one-clock capture-delay injection
  and around EOP/reset.
  **This is not a production change, a synchronizer decision, or a fix.**
  It does not claim:
  - USB compliance;
  - a clock-tolerance envelope;
  - any analog metastability, resolution-time or MTBF coverage. Every
    flop resolves cleanly in zero-delay RTL. The injection is a
    deterministic digital stand-in for unequal capture.

  The harness/control leg passes. The characterization legs record
  measured failures as failures. Sits beside, and does not supersede:
  - `20261008-210900-2875d57` (the DR-0003 independent-host matrix),
    which this record reproduces;
  - `20261008-235500-9e3008a` (synchronous functional behaviour).
- **Design provenance**: `rtl/usb_utmi_phy.v` and submodules @ `05087d5`.
  Their hashes are byte-identical to record `20261008-210900-2875d57`;
  this change does not touch `rtl/`. Candidate: `rtl` under test plus
  `verification/tb_usb_rx_sync_candidate.v`, which is new in this change
  and so is not in `05087d5`. A frozen copy is at
  `artifacts/20261009-123738-05087d5/candidate-source-tb_usb_rx_sync_candidate.v`,
  and its hash is in the metadata block.
- **Run configuration**:
  - Commands, run **sequentially**, one simulator process each:
    `USB_RXTOL_GRID=full USB_RXSYNC_DESIGNS=baseline USB_RXSYNC_OUT_DIR=<a> klt
    functional-verification verification/request-usb-rx-sync-candidate.json
    --format json`, then the same with `USB_RXSYNC_DESIGNS=candidate
    USB_RXSYNC_OUT_DIR=<b>`.
  - Tools: klt 0.7.0+gc857f2efb753 run from a throwaway `uvx` environment
    that adds cocotb 2.1.0 (the host's klt tool environment lacks
    cocotb), Icarus 13.0. Timescale 1ns/1ps, so the simulator tick is
    1 ps. `random_seed` 1, payload seed 1. Device clock period
    T = 83334 ps.
  - **Grid** (per design):
    - The existing `test_usb_rx_clock_tolerance.py` grid: offsets 0,
      +/-1000, +/-2500, +/-5000 ppm, plus the 1.0025/0.9975 and
      0.9975/1.0025 endpoint ratios.
    - Phases: the eight k/8-T phases (0 = coincident with a DUT rising
      edge), plus **+1 tick** and **T-1 tick** (one tick after and one
      before a rising edge).
    - Patterns all-ones / all-zeros / seeded-mixed; post-SYNC lengths
      1/8/64/1024 bytes.
    - That is 9×10×3×4 = 1080 cases per design. Stimulus, absolute-time
      scheduling and scoreboard are imported unchanged from the baseline
      harness.
  - **Skew sweep**:
    - Zero offset, 8 bytes, all 10 phases × 3 patterns, at skews 0,
      1 tick (1 ps), T/8 (10417 ps) and T/2 (41667 ps).
    - Transition type J->K or K->J, with DP leading or DM leading. The
      leading line switches at the scheduled host time and the other line
      skew later.
    - Scope: skew on every transition of that type ("all"), or only
      after SYNC ("payload").
    - Designs: baseline, candidate, and candidate with injection on DP,
      DM or both. 5 × 25 × 30 = 3750 cases.
  - **Injection model**: one extra clean capture flop on the named
    line(s), so that line reaches the PHY one clock later. Every stage
    resets synchronously to J (DP=1, DM=0) on `rst_n`=0 or UTMI `Reset`=1.
  - **Units**: phases and skews in ps; latencies in DUT rising edges;
    offsets in ppm (`1e6*(f_host/f_dev - 1)`).
  - **Coincidence**: every case records `coincident_host_edges`, the
    number of host line events landing exactly on a DUT rising edge.
    Their capture order is scheduler-dependent, so those cases are
    reported but **not claimed**. Phase 0 is always coincident. So is
    any phase + skew that equals T (e.g. phase 4/8 + T/2, phase 7/8 +
    T/8, phase T-1 + 1 tick).
- **Statistical convention**: deterministic; no randomness beyond the
  seeded mixed payload.
  - The skew sweep and controls were produced independently by both
    sequential runs. They are byte-identical, and were byte-identical
    again in a reduced run under CI's pins (klt e8ca621, cocotb 2.0.1).
  - Re-running the **unwrapped** baseline request
    `request-usb-rx-clock-tolerance.json` with `USB_RXTOL_GRID=full` at
    this commit reproduced the historical matrix artifact of
    `20261008-210900-2875d57` byte-for-byte (sha256 `48ae28ca…11cc`).
- **Result**:
  - **Harness and controls: PASS** (9/9 klt tests in each run).
    - The skew stimulus has the expected polarity: J->K with DP leading
      gives SE0, with DM leading gives SE1, and K->J is the reverse. The
      payload scope leaves SYNC unskewed.
    - Scoreboard negative controls: on real DUT runs with `RxError` low,
      an altered byte gives `corrupt_byte`, a missing byte `truncated`
      and an extra byte `extra_bytes`.
    - Reset: every candidate stage (s1, s2 and the injection stage s3)
      goes to J one edge after `rst_n`=0 or `Reset`=1, whether the line
      holds K, SE0 or SE1. `LineState` is J during reset. After release
      the chain refills in the expected number of edges.
    - Latency, measured from a mid-cycle input step to the first rising
      edge whose register shows it (the `usb_line_state_decode` register
      is included):

      | design | input -> s1 | s2 | PHY input | LineState |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | baseline | — | — | combinational | edge 1 |
      | candidate | edge 1 | edge 2 | edge 2 | edge 3 |
      | candidate + injection (injected line) | edge 1 | edge 2 | edge 3 | edge 4 |

      Packet status (the `RxActive` rise, every `RxValid` and the
      `RxActive` fall) is exactly baseline + 2 clocks for every pattern.
      For example, the mixed 8-byte control at phase 1/2:

      | design | `RxActive` rise | `RxValid` | `RxActive` fall | host SE0 start |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | baseline | edge 18 | 27, 35, …, 83 | edge 84 | edge 81 |
      | candidate | edge 20 | 29, …, 85 | edge 86 | edge 81 |

      Across the full grid the shift is +2 clocks for all three status
      edges in all 588 cases that pass in both designs.
    - Zero-skew, zero-offset, no-injection controls decode the exact
      bytes on both designs (3 patterns × 1/8/64 bytes, phase 1/2). A +5%
      host fails on both.
    - EOP: a real 2-bit SE0 EOP pulses `eop` once with no `bus_reset`.
    - Sustained bus reset: SE0 held for 40 bit times gives `bus_reset`
      for exactly 11 samples (from the 30th SE0 sample on), on both
      designs.
    - Recovery: in every scenario (bus reset after a packet, bus reset
      mid-packet, UTMI `Reset` in idle and mid-packet, `rst_n` in idle
      and mid-packet), on both designs, the **next** packet decodes
      exactly. The interrupted packet delivers 3 of 8 bytes with
      `RxError` low. That is recorded, not asserted.
    - Injection self-check: injection on DP alone manufactures 21 SE0 and
      22 SE1 samples, and on DM alone 22 and 21. Injection on both lines,
      and no injection, manufacture none.
  - **Grid (characterization)**: both designs **588/1080 receive-pass,
    492 measured failures**:
    - reasons: 482 `corrupt_byte`, 6 `extra_bytes`, 4 `truncated`;
    - 349 of the failures never raised `RxError`;
    - 108 cases are coincident and unclaimed. Excluding them, 522/972
      pass in both designs.

    Pass/fail is identical case-for-case between baseline and candidate.
    8 cases differ, but only in `received_bytes`, with the same reason
    and `RxError`. All 8 fail in both designs because `RxActive` never
    ends: a fast host shortens the EOP to a single SE0 sample, so the
    EOP is missed and the PHY keeps assembling idle-line garbage. The
    +2-clock latency moves one garbage byte out of the fixed observation
    window. That is an artifact of the observation window, not a
    behavioural difference.

    The wrapper-baseline subset at the eight k/8 phases equals the
    historical unwrapped matrix in all 864 cases (pass, reason,
    received bytes, `RxError`, first mismatch, `RxActive`). The
    candidate does **not** change DR-0003's result, and the
    synchronizer is not a frequency-slip fix. The +1-tick and T-1-tick
    phases (both within 1 ps of a rising edge) give the same result as
    the coincident phase 0 in every row. Phase tables (columns
    0, +1t, 1/8 … 7/8, −1t), the same for both designs:

    | offset | 1 B | 8 B | 64 B | 1024 B |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | 0 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPPP |
    | +1000 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPPP | PPFFFFPPPP | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | -1000 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPFFFFP | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | +2500 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPFPPPPPPP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | -2500 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPPPFP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | +5000 | PPFPPPPPPP | PPFFFPPPPP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | -5000 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPFFFP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | +5012.531 | PPFPPPPPPP | PPFFFPPPPP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |
    | -4987.531 | PPPPPPPPPP | PPPPPPFFFP | FFFFFFFFFF | FFFFFFFFFF |

  - **Skew × injection (characterization)**: full table in
    `artifacts/…/summary.txt`; the per-sample `LineState` run-length
    string for every case is in `skew-injection-sweep.json`.
    - **Polarity holds in sampled data.** The manufactured state is SE0
      for J->K with DP leading or K->J with DM leading, and SE1 for the
      other two. A manufactured run never exceeds 1 sample without
      injection, so no premature `eop` (it needs 2 SE0 samples) and no
      `bus_reset` occurred in any skew-only case.
    - **Skew-only, baseline vs candidate.** Excluding coincident cases,
      the two have identical results at every skew. Of 30 cases:

      | skew | passes, excluding coincident |
      |---|---|
      | 0 | 27/27 |
      | 1 tick | 24/24 |
      | T/8 | 21/24 |
      | T/2 | 12/24 |

      All 48 baseline-vs-candidate differences are coincident cases.
      Independent chains add no protection against inter-line skew, and
      no extra harm. A transition is mis-captured exactly when the
      sampling edge falls between the two line edges, in either design.
    - **Consequences.** With "all" scope a manufactured sample hits
      SYNC, and the packet is **dropped** (`no_rx_active`, no bytes).
      With "payload" scope the result is **corrupt bytes**. In 40 of the
      120 failing baseline payload-skew cases, and 32 of the 96
      candidate ones, `RxError` stayed low with 7 or 8 bytes delivered:
      silent corruption.
    - **Injection on one line: 0/750 pass per line.** Every packet is
      dropped at SYNC, because with a one-clock inter-line skew every
      J<->K transition becomes an SE0 or SE1 sample. With DM injected,
      the 2-sample host EOP after a final K shrinks to one SE0 sample:
      DM's fall arrives a clock late, while DP's rise to J is on time.
      Every 8-byte payload here ends on K, so `eop` never fired in any
      of the 30 zero-skew cases.
    - **Injection on both lines** behaves identically to the candidate,
      +1 clock.
  - **Overall: experiment valid (controls PASS).**
    - The candidate is structurally a pure +2-clock delay. It neither
      improves nor worsens the clock-offset grid (measured failures
      unchanged: 492/1080 per design).
    - Per-line independent chains manufacture SE0/SE1 whenever the two
      lines are captured on different clocks. This can come from input
      skew straddling an edge (as in the baseline), or from the chains
      resolving on different clocks (modelled by injection, fatal to
      every packet here).
  - **Open questions for DR-0003 / #126** (not decided here):
    1. Whether any synchronizer is needed at all, given that DR-0003's
       sampling architecture (e.g. oversampling, Option A) would replace
       the sampler that the synchronizer feeds.
    2. If one is needed: coherent paired capture versus independent
       chains, or a one-sample SE0/SE1 qualifier. A qualifier changes
       EOP/reset criteria and would need its own spec decision.
    3. Which clock domain and owner (the wrapper or the analog/digital
       integration).
    4. How to bound analog inter-line skew at the receiver outputs.
  - Any later production integration must reassess the
    place-and-route, STA and gate-level records. This verification-only
    change does not invalidate them.
